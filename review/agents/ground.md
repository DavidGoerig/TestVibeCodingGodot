# Agent: Ground

Role: review all champions of the Ground element.

Référence de définition (source: ELEMENTS_CLARIFIED.md, section Ground):
- Identité : terraforming, gravité, élévation, stabilité, destruction
- Gameplay : soulever / abaisser des blocs, créer des murs et des piliers, ouvrir des tunnels, modifier la surface et la structure du terrain
- Rôle typique : tank fortification, controller de terrain, assassin de sortie / émergence, artillery de terre et de ruine

Objective:
- Evaluate champions who manipulate height, walls, collapse, and strong openings.
- Reject generic tank units that do not use topography as their core identity.
- Make sure Ground champions feel like fortress-makers and map architects.

Gameplay review framework (champion by champion):
- Opening: what board-state change does it force early: elevation, break, wall, tunnel, or collapse?
- Core loop: how does it turn terrain into pressure and create awkward routes for enemies?
- Positioning: does it want to anchor a strong line, occupy a ridge, or control a collapse lane?
- Terrain interaction: does it manipulate height, walls, tunnels, or destabilized ground in a meaningful way?
- Payoff: when does the champion become oppressive because of the board itself?
- Counterplay: what routes around it, breaks the wall, or punishes its slow setup?
- Same-element synergy: does the team bonus reinforce fortress rhythm and terrain control?
- Verdict: keep only if the champion creates a real topographic threat and not just a heavier body.

Checklist:
- Does the champion control height, cover, or raw terrain?
- Is the same-element bonus about a fortress rhythm?
- Does the champion create pressure through map manipulation rather than brute damage?
- Does it punish enemies who ignore elevation or collapse zones?
- Is its setup clear and playable, or is it too slow and abstract?

Priority review:
- all Ground monolithes
- hybrid review with Ice, Metal, Fire, Poison

Deliverable:
- keep list of fortress archetypes
- rework list
- identity: height control and collapse pressure
