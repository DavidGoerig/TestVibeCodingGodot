# Terrain and Element Rules

## Terrain types

The terrain should be readable and useful.

### Common tile types
- ground
- wall / block
- cover
- elevated platform
- shallow water
- lava / heated tile
- grass / bush
- snow / ice
- stone / fortified tile
- poison tile
- shadow tile
- electric tile
- healing tile
- buff tile
- resource tile

## Elemental behavior

### Fire
- burn tiles
- pressure on adjacent cells
- dangerous when enemies stay in place
- ideal for lane threat and burst decks

### Water
- slows movement
- allows rerouting
- creates flooded paths and line control
- useful for lane pressure and displacement

### Grass
- cover and stealth-safe tiles
- healing or sustain zones
- good for ambush and rotate play

### Ice
- slippery movement
- route control and flow disruption
- stronger for lane locking and lane denial

### Ground / Stone
- fortified tiles
- high-ground potential
- strong cover and lane denial

### Electric
- conductive tile chain logic
- risks enemy grouping or line stacking
- punishes poor spacing

### Poison
- toxic duration effect
- lane attrition and hazard pressure
- strong as a slow pressure terrain

### Shadow
- obscured vision and stealth windows
- good for ambush and flanking decisions

### Metal
- barrier-esque route management
- reinforced and dangerous tiles
- good for lane control and wall logic

### Normal
- neutral adaptation tiles
- improvised objects
- flexible terrain support

## Progression tiles

Every progression tile should answer three questions:
- what is the reward?
- what is the risk?
- who can use it best?

Examples:
- heal tile
- mana gain tile
- power buff tile
- move speed tile
- cover tile
- lane control tile
- objective trigger tile

## Visual rule

Terrain must be readable from a distance.

A strong prototype uses:
- simple materials
- obvious color coding
- clear damage or buff icons
- strong contrast between terrain states
